# [Take the Vegetable Survey](https://vegetable.glimpsegaze.com)

**The Great Vegetable Survey** is a short questionnaire about a word that feels obvious until you try to define it. It is a research prototype, not a botany test. There are no wrong answers. The point is to record what people actually mean by *vegetable*: the first thing that comes to mind, the reasons they give, how they sort borderline cases, and the definition they are willing to write down.

The survey moves through that sequence in a few minutes. After a first instinct, it asks why some foods count and others do not, then offers a ranking of awkward cases — things that are roots, flowers, seeds, or botanical fruits, and still sit somewhere on a kitchen table. An optional note at the end leaves room for background that does not fit the questions.

The results turn those answers into a **vegetableness spectrum**. Each item, from sweet potato to green bean, can be placed from least to most vegetable, with a short note on why the case is awkward. A completed survey can be set against that spread, so a personal ranking sits next to the wider pattern instead of disappearing into a private form.

The project treats a folk category as something you can explore, argue with, and compare, rather than as a fact to be memorised.
